

The WDM transceiver consists of four independent optical channels operating at distinct wavelengths. Each channel is generated by a continuous-wave (CW) laser source and modulated with pseudo-random binary sequence (PRBS) data using a Mach–Zehnder Modulator (MZM). The modulated optical signals are combined using an optical power combiner and transmitted through a common silicon waveguide, forming a wavelength-division multiplexed (WDM) signal.

At the receiver, the multiplexed optical signal is demultiplexed using a cascade of microring resonators. Each microring is designed with a specific radius to align its resonance wavelength with one of the transmitted channels. When a channel's wavelength matches the resonance condition of a ring, the optical power is coupled from the bus waveguide to the ring's drop port, allowing the original wavelength channels to be separated and recovered independently.

The complete system was modeled and verified in ANSYS Lumerical, demonstrating wavelength-selective routing and recovery of multiple data channels through microring-resonator-based demultiplexing.
